DEI Audit in India: What It Covers, How It Works, and Why It Matters

DEI audits in India are having a moment. Every large organisation seems to have one on the agenda. But what most companies actually get is a survey, a report, and not much else. A real DEI Audit in India gets under the surface — into how leaders actually behave, how ground-level employees experience the culture, and whether your office can even be accessed by someone in a wheelchair.

Most organisations in India now have a DEI policy written somewhere. Whether anyone is actually living it is a different question entirely.

That gap — between what a policy says and what employees experience on a Tuesday afternoon — is what a DEI Audit in India is built to find. Not through assumptions, not through leadership's best guess, but through structured, validated measurement at every level of the organisation.

Ask Insights has been doing this work in India for over two decades. The companies we have worked with include Google, Hindustan Unilever, PepsiCo and Amazon. In that time, one thing has stayed consistent: the organisations that benefit most from an audit are not the ones that expected bad news. They are the ones that were genuinely surprised by what they found.

What Does a DEI Audit in India Actually Look At?

Most people assume a DEI audit is a headcount exercise. Count the women in leadership, check the disability hiring numbers, file the report. That is not what we do.

What we measure is experience. How included do people actually feel in this organisation — not in theory, but in the meetings they attend, the feedback they receive, the promotions they do or do not get? Do managers at the N-1 level demonstrate inclusive behaviour, or just talk about it in town halls? Is the culture your CEO describes the same culture your entry-level employees are living in?

A thorough Diversity and Inclusion Audit in India covers:

  • Leadership behaviour and DEI competence at the senior and N-1 levels

  • Day-to-day workplace culture experienced by individual contributors across functions

  • Whether organisational policies are equitable in practice, not just in writing

  • Representation across gender, generation, LGBTQIA+ identity and disability

  • Physical infrastructure — can a person with a disability actually navigate your office independently?

Each of these needs a different measurement tool. That is why a single survey cannot do what a proper Workplace Inclusion Audit in India does.

The Ask Insights DEI Assessment Framework

Inclusion works differently at different levels of an organisation. What a senior leader experiences is not what a junior employee experiences. Both matter. But measuring them with the same instrument gives you neither picture accurately.

Our framework has four tiers — each designed for a different layer of the workforce.

360 DEI Assessment for Senior Leaders

We start with leadership, because that is where inclusion either takes root or does not.

For the senior team, we run a comprehensive DEI survey followed by a 90-minute working debrief — led by Ask Insights' founding team, who are among the earliest DEI practitioners in India. The debrief is not a slideshow of results. It is a direct conversation about what the data means, what leadership is not seeing, and what needs to change at the top before anything else can shift.

Leadership buy-in is the single biggest predictor of whether a DEI strategy succeeds or stays on a slide deck. This session is designed to create it.

Psychometric Assessment for the N-1 Tier

The management layer below the C-suite is where most DEI initiatives either gain traction or quietly die.

For this group, we use a validated psychometric instrument that assigns each leader a DEI competence level — from 'Starting' through to 'Mentoring'. This is not a self-assessment where people rate themselves highly and move on. It is a calibrated tool designed to surface the gap between what leaders intend and what they actually do in practice.

The output tells you exactly where your management bench stands and which development paths will actually move things forward.

Culture and Climate Assessment for Individual Contributors

Ground-level employees are often the most honest source of information about what an organisation's culture is really like. They experience it daily, without the filter that comes with seniority.

Our culture and climate instrument evaluates DEI across 10 focused categories, producing a statistically reliable picture of how inclusion is experienced at the working level. This is where the gap between policy and reality shows up most clearly — and where organisations are most often surprised.

Cultural Dipstick

Not every organisation needs a full multi-tier audit straight away.

The Cultural Dipstick is a faster, targeted assessment for organisations that want to take the pulse of their inclusion culture before committing to a larger intervention. It works particularly well before a strategy refresh, a significant restructure, or a leadership transition.

It finds things that would otherwise take months of observation to identify — patterns, blind spots, and pockets of exclusion that do not appear in standard HR data.

Infrastructure Accessibility Audit for Organisations in India

Inclusion is not just a mindset question. It is also a physical one.

Can a person who uses a wheelchair get into your building without assistance? Can they reach the bathroom, the cafeteria, their desk? If the answer is no — or if you are not sure — your organisation has an inclusion problem that no amount of training will solve.

Our Accessibility Audit for Organisations in India is a detailed on-site evaluation of your built environment, assessed against the Revised National Building Code 2016 and the CPWD Manual on Barrier-Free Accessibility. It is also aligned with the Rights of Persons with Disabilities (RPWD) Act 2016, which places a specific legal obligation on establishments to provide accessible infrastructure.

The audit is led by International Para Athlete Ms. Suvarna Raj alongside a specialist team. Every finding comes with photographs, measurements and specific remediation steps. Not general suggestions — actual instructions for what needs to change and where.

For organisations that want disability inclusion to mean something beyond a line in the annual report, this is where that work begins.
